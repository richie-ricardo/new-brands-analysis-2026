Where the dollars are coming from

Net sales by brand, largest first. The program is carrying a heavy concentration in its top handful of names.

Nike alone accounts for 30.6% of new-brand gross sales ($1.79M of $5.86M) — more than the next three brands combined. Widen the lens slightly and it gets sharper: the top 5 brands (Nike, Pas Normal Studios, Rhythm, Camper, Crocs) — just 12% of the 41-brand roster — generate 62.9% of gross sales. The remaining 36 brands split the other third.

That's not necessarily a problem — Nike is doing exactly what a flagship launch should — but it does mean portfolio-level KPIs (blended margin, blended sellthru) are effectively a read on a handful of brands, not the cohort as a whole. The next section looks at the tier just below the top 5, which is a more representative read on the broader program.

Channel mix varies widely underneath that top-line number. Most top-15 brands lean heavily on ALS, with TLH contributing a single-digit share of net sales — Nike's TLH share is just 9.3%, for example. Rhythm is the clear exception: its TLH channel (52.3% of its net sales) actually out-earns ALS, worth factoring into channel-specific buy and marketing decisions for that brand specifically.

Risk  DL1961 is the standout outlier in the whole portfolio: 4.8% sellthru against 4,635 units received — the lowest of any brand by a wide margin, and far below sibling launches from the same window (Matador 72.8%, Malbon 24.1%, both live since February 2026). Combined with a 43.1% unit refund rate — the second-highest in the program — this looks less like "still ramping" and more like a fit, sizing, or buy-quantity issue specific to the brand. SUBU (5.7% sellthru) shows a milder version of the same pattern.

Risk  Isadore (19.4% net margin) and Purple Mountain Observatory (20.8%) sit well below every other brand doing comparable volume — the next-lowest margin is Tracksmith at 32.3%. Both are established brands (12+ months live) with decent sellthru, so this reads as a cost or pricing issue rather than a slow start.

Strong  Owala, Matador, Crocs, and Ruffwear are the cleanest performers in the portfolio — each sits above the blended average on both margin and sellthru simultaneously, meaning the assortment is selling through and holding margin. Camper is close behind: sellthru well above average (69.2%) with margin just a point under it (39.8%). SOAR Running and Malbon take the opposite trade: excellent margin (57.8% and 50.7%) but sellthru still under 25% — premium positioning that hasn't found volume yet.

04 — TLH's role in combined sellthru

Why some combined sellthru numbers look low

Combined sellthru is ALS's contribution plus TLH's contribution (units received aren't tracked separately by channel, so this is the cleanest split available). Normally ALS carries most of it — but not always.

ALS vs. TLH contribution to combined sellthru
21 of 41 brands have no TLH orders yet — combined sellthru for them is entirely ALS. Shown here: the 20 brands with real TLH activity, sorted by TLH's share of the total.
ALS
TLH

Of the 20 brands with any TLH volume, ALS still contributes more than TLH in 14 — consistent with TLH generally being the smaller channel for a new brand. But six brands buck that pattern, with TLH actually contributing more to combined sellthru than ALS: Rhythm, Tracksmith, Purple Mountain Observatory, Berghaus, Skechers, and MINI A TURE. For those, an ALS-only read would understate how the brand is really performing.

Most of the portfolio's lowest combined-sellthru brands don't have a TLH story at all — 21 brands have zero TLH orders to date, so their number is purely ALS. DL1961 is the clearest case where a weak TLH contribution (1.2%) compounds an already-weak ALS number (3.6%) rather than masking a stronger one underneath. The cleaner examples of TLH specifically holding back an otherwise solid brand are Pas Normal Studios (42.5% on ALS, just 1.9% on TLH, for 44.3% combined) and Velocio (24.0% on ALS, 5.4% on TLH, for 29.4% combined) — both would sit noticeably higher on an ALS-only view.

05 — Reading sellthru fairly

Time explains a lot of the sellthru gap

Brands launched at wildly different times — comparing a 2-week-old launch to Nike's 20-month run isn't apples to apples. Grouping by tenure shows why.

Avg. sellthru % and margin % by brand tenure
41 brands grouped into four launch-tenure cohorts.
Avg. sellthru %
Avg. net margin %

Sellthru climbs steadily with tenure — from a 9.4% average in brands live under 3 months, to 30.1% at 3–6 months, 38.7% at 6–12 months, and 55.7% for brands live over a year. The correlation between days-live and sellthru across all 41 brands is 0.72 — one of the strongest relationships in this data.

The practical read: the six brands live under 3 months (Samsøe Samsøe, Eastside Golf, ELLIKER, Rapha, Left On Friday, Hermanos Koumori) have single-digit sellthru almost by construction, not because they're underperforming. Margin tells the opposite story — it's highest in the newest cohort (49.1%) and drifts down slightly as brands mature (41.3% past 12 months), consistent with early full-price sell-through before markdown cycles start. So the portfolio's blended sellthru figure is, in part, just a reflection of how long these brands have collectively been on shelf.

06 — Margin bridge

Where the margin leaks

Of $5.86M in gross sales across the portfolio, here's what survives to net margin — and what each step of the bridge costs.

Gross sales to net margin, full portfolio
Blue = running total, amber = the deduction at that step.

Refunds take the single largest bite before cost is even considered: $1.22M of gross sales is given back, before COGS. Cost of goods removes a further $2.20M, and the cost recovered (or not) on returned units costs another $546K in margin — landing the portfolio at $1.89M net margin, a 32.3% conversion rate from gross sales.

Watch  Nudie Jeans (46.1%) and DL1961 (43.1%) carry the two highest unit refund rates in the portfolio by a wide margin over the next-highest (Camper, 32.1%) — both are denim brands, which points at a fit/sizing pattern specific to the category rather than two unrelated issues. Together they account for $48.4K of the portfolio's $546K in refund-driven margin loss.
